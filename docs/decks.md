## Modes

- **Ledger sync** (default): reads every row of a Notion database (the "Language Learning Ledger") and creates or updates one Anki `Basic` note per row.
- **Kana seed** (`--seed-kana`): writes the built-in hiragana and katakana tables (104 each: gojūon, dakuten/handakuten, yōon) to `Nihongo::Kana::Hiragana` and `Nihongo::Kana::Katakana`.
- **Kanji seed** (`--seed-kanji`): writes the 1,000 most frequent kanji from `kanji_top1000.tsv`, then the rows of `kanji_extra.tsv`, to `Nihongo::Core Kanji`, in frequency order. It skips kanji that already have a ledger card. Ledger `Kanji` rows also live in `Nihongo::Core Kanji` (`TYPE_DECKS`; merged on 2026-10-27, so `Nihongo::Kanji` is now unused). Inside that deck, a `notion_` link tag marks a ledger note, and its absence marks a seed note.

- **Verb seed** (`--seed-verbs`): writes the verbs in `verbs.tsv` to `Nihongo::Verbs`, in file (book) order. A verb with a ledger card in `Nihongo::Vocab` takes it over (see "One owner per word" below).

- **Word seed** (`--seed-words`): writes the words in `words.tsv` to `Nihongo::Words`, in file order (added 2026-11-03). `seeded_words` drops two kinds of row: verbs that are in `verbs.tsv`, and one-kanji words their Core Kanji card covers (`kanji_card_covers`: the word's kana is the kanji's main bold reading, the first `*` on its On or Kun line; 147 on 2026-11-03, while 9 such as 後 あと keep a card). It adopts the matching `Nihongo::Vocab` ledger card (`adopt=(VOCAB_SCOPE, WORD_DECK, str.strip)`), so the card moves to Words, loses its `notion_`/`lessonN`/`vocab` tags and keeps its history. The verb seed adopts from Vocab the same way.

- **Numbers seed** (`--seed-numbers`, added 2026-11-06): writes `numbers.tsv` to `Nihongo::Numbers & Time` (one deck; the user wanted one subdeck, with groups as tags rather than sub-subdecks). Cards are expressions in real-world formats, Arabic digits as usually written (`3本`, `4階`, `午後2時`, `2:30 PM`, `3月14日`, `¥1,980`); the Back's bold headline is the reading, because reading the format is the skill. Columns: `group` (validated against `NUMBER_GROUPS`; becomes a tag next to `numbers`), `front`, `kana`, `meaning`, `note`. Hand-made, N5/N4. Single vocabulary words (一人, 二日, 去年, 毎日, weekdays, 〜本) stay in `words.tsv`; Fronts don't collide because Words uses kanji numerals. `tools/check_data.py` checks each small number + counter (3本 → 三本) against JMdict's readings (77 of 161 rows on 2026-11-06); clock times, prices and phrases are compositional and checked by hand.
- **Kanji look-alikes seed** (`--seed-lookalikes`, added 2026-11-14, user's request: compare learned kanji with similar radicals/components): `Nihongo::Kanji Look-alikes`, one card per pair (user chose pairs over groups), Front `待・持`, Back = per kanji its first two meanings, bold (*) On/Kun readings and radical line (`radicals_lines`, shown even for its own radical, like Verbs), then a faded `Same: … · Differs: 持 has 扌, 待 has 彳` line (`_telling_parts`: single strokes dropped when a bigger part exists; a kanji isn't its own part; no parts left = no Differs; identical parts = "Same parts, different arrangement"). Pairs live in `lookalikes.tsv` (columns a, b, same, a_only, b_only; 1,631 pairs over all Core Kanji), built by `tools/add_lookalikes.py --write` from siikamiika/similar-kanji (MIT, commit pinned in `SIMILAR_COMMIT`) and KRADFILE parts; rerun it after adding Core Kanji rows. The user chose "only studied kanji": the seed adds a pair only when both kanji's Core Kanji cards are non-new (`studied_kanji`, so it needs Anki open even for `--dry-run`), and each sync adds pairs newly completed (69 on 2026-11-14). Tag `lookalike`. Run it in every sync, after the other seeds.

- **Study order** (`--order`, added 2026-11-11): Words runs in word-frequency order and Core Kanji in kanji-frequency order, so 71% of the words studied by 2026-11-11 used a kanji not yet studied (kanji, reading and meaning learned at once; the Words review pass rate was 41%). `--order` runs `order_kanji` then `order_words`. `order_kanji` moves the new Core Kanji cards whose kanji appear in a studied Words, Verbs or Numbers & Time card (`STUDIED_WORD_DECKS`) to the front, tagged `word_seen` (`SEEN_TAG`). `order_words` moves the new Words cards whose kanji all have a studied Core Kanji card, and kana-only words, to the front, tagged `kanji_ready` (`READY_TAG`). Since 2026-11-12 `order_kanji` also moves, right after those, the `NEXT_KANJI` (50) new kanji that the next new Words cards still need, in the order the words come up, tagged `word_next` (`NEXT_TAG`); without it, kanji outside the frequency top 1,000 (the `kanji_extra.tsv` ones, 猫 雪) sat at the end of the queue and held their words back for months. `_front_load` takes `[(tag, cards)]` groups in priority order and moves the last group first. Each group keeps its existing relative order (so N5/N4 kanji stay first) and untag cards that no longer qualify; the seeds don't own these tags. AnkiConnect refuses to set a card's `due` ("Use native scheduling commands for protected state") and has no reposition action, so the move goes through **the helper add-on in `anki_addon/notes_to_anki_helper/`**: it serves Anki's own `sched.reposition_new_cards` (randomize off, shift existing) on `127.0.0.1:8766` (`HELPER_URL`), only for new cards, as an undoable `CollectionOp` with no review-log entries. Install it once per machine as a symlink in Anki's `addons21` folder (README); it loads when Anki starts. Installed on the Mac on 2026-11-11 (at the user's request); the Windows PC still needs it. Without it, `_front_load` opens the cards in the browser for a manual Cards > Reposition. Since 2026-11-14 (user's request) `--order` also runs `order_verbs`: new Verbs cards whose kanji are all studied (or kana-only) go first, tagged `kanji_ready`, then the rest; within each part godan and ichidan alternate (`_alternate`), so a godan verb ending in る (入る, 切る) comes next to real ichidan ones (いる, 着る), and irregular verbs keep their place at the end. Each group keeps its book order. Until `ICHIDAN_LEAD` (5) ichidan verbs are studied, the missing ones go first (user's request, 2026-11-14: 60 godan studied, no ichidan yet), so the lead ends by itself. This is a full reorder, not a front-load, so it uses the helper's `setNewOrder` action (add-on version 2: the cards swap their own queue positions, other cards don't move, one undo step); with an older add-on it skips the move and says to restart Anki. Version 2 was loaded on the Mac on 2026-11-14. Also since 2026-11-14 (user's request), when `tools/cache/examples.utf.gz` exists (downloaded by `tools/readable_sentences.py`), `unlock_words` finds the `UNLOCK_WORDS` (20) words that would each make the most Tatoeba sentences readable (new Words cards, and one-kanji words a new Core Kanji card teaches, like 彼); `order_kanji` puts the kanji they need first (tag `unlocks`, before `word_seen`), and `order_words` puts the ready ones first (tag `unlocks`, before `kanji_ready`). Without the file that step is skipped (the Windows PC). The sentence parsing and matching (`example_sentences`, `WordSet`, `studied_words`, `sentence_unlocks`) live in `main.py` and the tool imports them. Run `--order` during each sync. Recommended alongside it: Core Kanji 10 new/day and Words 3 (Core Kanji shares the `Default` preset with Verbs and Numbers & Time, so it needs its own preset); re-check the Words pass rate around 2026-11-25 before splitting Words into reading and meaning cards.

- **Sentence seed** (`--seed-sentences`, added 2026-11-14, user's request): writes `sentences.tsv` to `Nihongo::Sentences`, in file order, skipping rows whose note is `skip`. Front = the Japanese sentence; Back (`sentence_back`) = the English as headline, one line per word (`行こう → 行く いく · to go`, glosses from `words.tsv`/`verbs.tsv` via `_word_glosses`), the note, a faded `Grammar:` line (particles/copula) and `Tatoeba #<jpn id> · CC BY 2.0 FR`. Tag `sentence`. Columns: `id` (Tatoeba `jpn_eng`), `japanese`, `english`, `words` (`front` or `front{form used}`), `grammar`, `note`. Rows come from `tools/add_sentences.py` (dry run by default, `--write` appends, `--max` default 40, shortest first): sentences whose every word is studied (`studied_words`) and every kanji studied, 5-30 characters, not already listed, and not a one-word variant of a listed or picked one (`skeletons`, so 私は医者です/私は学生です don't both go in). No content filter: the user chose to keep Tatoeba's crude or violent lines. Rerun it after study progress, then `--seed-sentences`. Started with 100 rows on 2026-11-14 (398 qualified); the deck uses the `Nihongo Vocab` preset (5 new/day), set via `setDeckConfigId`.

## Words, verbs and kanji: data notes

**Topics** (added 2026-11-06). `words.tsv` column 7 `topic` (19 topics, one per word, ~460 words; abstract words have none) becomes a `topic_<name>` tag (`TOPIC_TAG_PREFIX`). `kanji_topics` gives a Core Kanji card the topic of the one-kanji word it covers (83 kanji), since the word seed skips those words. Tags starting `topic_` are script-owned, so a changed topic replaces the old tag. The initial assignment came from `tools/topics_data.py` via `tools/assign_topics.py` (which only rewrites rows whose topic changes, and appended 10 everyday non-JLPT words with a blank level: ピンク オレンジ 紫 灰色 金色 銀色 肩 胸 膝 腰); afterwards edit topics in `words.tsv` directly. The user studies a topic via Custom Study by tag, not separate decks (rejected: moving hundreds of cards and splitting the new-card order).

**One owner per word.** A ledger `Vocab` row whose Item is in `seeded_words()` or `verbs.tsv` is skipped by `sync_ledger` (one summary line) and its link tag goes in `keep`, so `--prune` doesn't delete the card before the seed adopts it. New Notion words outside the files still sync to `Nihongo::Vocab`. On 2026-11-03, 60 rows were handed over: 54 words, plus 6 verbs (聞く 読む 飲む 話す 書く 行く) whose Vocab cards duplicate existing Verbs cards and must be deleted by hand once. 洗う 寝る 見る 帰る 食べる 来る were to stay in Vocab until the book's ichidan/irregular section adds them to `verbs.tsv`, but on 2026-11-09 they had no card anywhere in Anki, and the empty `Nihongo::Vocab` deck was deleted (user's request). `words.tsv` is generated data (columns `level` n5/n4/blank, `word`, `kana`, `meaning`, `type`, `note`). The first word using each kana feature the kana decks lack (small っ/ッ, ー, ティ, ファ…) gets an explanation in its note. The generator was never committed and is lost; it matched kana-only words to JMdict by reading alone, so 10 kana-only rows got a homonym's part of speech (はく as 伯/箔 "noun" instead of 履く, そこ with 底's "bottom, sole" gloss). **Radicals** (added 2026-11-07). Both kanji files have a 9th column `radical`, `no|form` (Kangxi number and the shape in that kanji: 海 `85|氵`, 泉 `85|水`), set by `tools/add_radicals.py` from KANJIDIC2's classical radical, with KRADFILE's parts picking the variant form (its stand-ins like 汁 = 氵 are mapped in `VARIANTS`). `radicals.tsv` (181 rows: no, form, name, meaning, kanji count) holds one row per form used; names and meanings were written by hand on 2026-11-07 (meanings started from the Unicode radical names). `kanji_back` adds a faded `Radical: 氵 さんずい · water` line above the strokes line (`radical_line`). A kanji that is its own radical (97 cards: 白 自 人 日…) was left without it until 2026-11-14, when the user asked for it; now it reads `Radical: 白 しろ · white (this kanji is a radical itself)`. The user decided against a separate radical deck for now (N4 doesn't test radicals; the count column is there to order one later, around N3). Note the official radical can differ from the visible part (問 and 聞 are filed under 口 and 耳, not 門).

`kanji_top1000.tsv` is generated data. It was built once from KANJIDIC2 (CC BY-SA 4.0: frequency, readings, meanings, strokes) and kanji-data (MIT: JLPT N-levels and WaniKani's reading order). Its `#` header lists the sources and formatting rules. A leading `*` marks each kanji's most common on/kun reading, which comes first in its list. Since 2026-11-09 (user's request: the kanji card faded readings their Words/Verbs cards teach, e.g. 後 あと) any other reading on the line that an N5/N4 word in `words.tsv` or a verb in `verbs.tsv` uses is also `*` and follows it (49 lines). `kanji_readings` renders the leading `*` run bold and fades the rest. By hand that day: 後's duplicate うしろ removed, and 何's lone on'yomi カ unmarked (faded; it's almost never used). A line with no marked reading is faded entirely, even a lone reading (since 2026-10-29; before, a lone reading was bold, which made rare ones like 万's よろず look essential): about 133 kun lines and 5 on lines. The 8th column, `example` (`word|kana|gloss|level`), is a word that reads the kanji with one of its on'yomi; `example_word` shows it, with the word's JLPT level, under the On line. It's the easiest JLPT word (N5 first, from open-anki-jlpt-decks, MIT, built on Jonathan Waller's CC BY lists), the main on'yomi winning ties; with no JLPT word, the most frequent common JMdict word (EDRDG, CC BY-SA 4.0) and a blank level. Only 370 of the 1,156 example words also have their own card (Words/Verbs/Numbers); the rest are mostly N3+ (飲食店) and are deliberately not carded. They're there to show the reading. Revisit once N5/N4 Words is done (user, 2026-11-14). A one-kanji word its kanji card teaches (父, 城) has its `words.tsv` note shown on the kanji card (`kanji_word_notes`, 2026-11-14). JmdictFurigana (CC BY-SA 4.0) says which kana belong to which kanji. Across both files (1,017 kanji, 2026-10-29): 156 N5, 149 N4, 442 N3, 180 N2/N1, 70 unlevelled, and 20 kanji (mostly name kanji like 藤, 岡) with none. The generators were never committed and are lost.
